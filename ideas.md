# 9XM Morning Playlist — design direction

## Three possible approaches

### Theme Name: Cable Sunrise
Very Brief Intro: A warm, broadcast-era morning mood built from CRT glow, saffron light, maroon plastic, and handwritten TV-guide details. The page feels like finding the remote before school and landing on the perfect song.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Bollywood Mixtape Desk
Very Brief Intro: A tactile desktop of CD sleeves, scribbled track lists, and glossy magazine color, with the playlist treated like a lovingly assembled mixtape. The emotion is intimate, collected, and personal.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Neon Music Player
Very Brief Intro: A darker late-night music-channel world with electric accents, scanlines, and a digital broadcast console. The feeling is more club-like and kinetic than morning-soft.
Probability: 0.02

## Chosen approach: Cable Sunrise

### Design Movement
Postmodern broadcast graphics meets Indian cable-TV nostalgia: early-2000s channel bugs, oversaturated music-video color, CRT glass, and imperfect analog texture reinterpreted with editorial restraint.

### Core Principles
The experience should feel like a recovered television memory rather than a generic streaming page. Every surface should carry a little evidence of broadcast life: scanlines, timing labels, stickers, signal noise, or a tiny program note. Layout should be asymmetrical and staged like a TV set in a lived-in room, while the interactions should stay crisp and modern.

### Color Philosophy
The foundation is a deep ink-indigo, like the room before the sun fully arrives. Saffron and turmeric yellow bring in the warmth of Indian morning light; faded maroon recalls old TV plastics and Bollywood title cards; mint/chartreuse is reserved for live signal details and playful contrast. The signature brand color is **signal saffron #F7B538**: bright, optimistic, and unmistakably broadcast.

### Layout Paradigm
A cinematic split composition: a left editorial rail carries the broadcast metadata and emotional copy, while a large tilted television object anchors the right side. The lower player behaves like a translucent glass control deck that floats across both worlds. On small screens, the layout stacks into a poster-like sequence without losing the TV-as-hero relationship.

### Signature Elements
The central object is a rounded CRT television with a warm screen, antenna, chunky side buttons, and a tiny 9XM-style channel bug. A thin broadcast ticker runs across the composition with snippets such as “Bakwaas Bandh Kar” and “signal found.” The player uses frosted glass, a luminous saffron progress line, and a compact waveform made from irregular bars.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like operating a beloved old remote: direct, tactile, and slightly playful. The play button is the primary switch. Arrow keys change the track state, while pressing **M** triggers a brief “Bakwaas Bandh Kar” signal burst and visual static.

### Animation
Entrance motion is slow and cinematic: the TV settles into place with a slight physical tilt, while small metadata labels reveal in staggered timing. The screen has a barely perceptible luminance pulse, the ticker drifts continuously, and the waveform gently breathes only when playing. Button feedback is fast and physical, never floaty. Non-essential motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use **Bebas Neue** for broadcast headlines and labels, with **DM Sans** for UI copy and **IBM Plex Mono** for timestamps, track metadata, and signal readouts. Large display type should be condensed, stacked, and slightly irregular in rhythm; body copy stays compact and readable.

### Brand Essence
A morning broadcast memory for anyone who learned Bollywood songs from the TV before streaming — vivid, familiar, and made for one more replay. Personality: **warm, cheeky, cinematic**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines should sound like a channel ident or a note from a friend who remembers the same song. CTAs are short, specific, and a little playful. Example lines: “Press play. Let the room remember.” and “Aaj ka signal: dil se.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark is a compact stacked “9 / XM” signal mark: a large outlined 9 intersecting a small offset XM block, as if stamped onto a CRT corner. It should be drawn with CSS/SVG geometry rather than typed as a default wordmark.

### Signature Brand Color
**Signal saffron #F7B538**.
